1. A deliberate **whole-day less-risky bias candidate** with cross-session robustness and explicit bias-lock confidence.
2. Explicit **top-four full-row highlighting** in the main rank table.
3. A dedicated **eight-session multi-symbol table** with at least the best two symbols per session and the best two sessions per symbol.
4. A dedicated **sentiment/news event ranking table** with event time, current time, event age, impact remaining, absorption state, novelty, surprise, pair relevance, and event-risk permission.

# 2. Twenty research papers and direct Field 10 use

## Paper 1 — James D. Hamilton (1989)

**Exact title:** *A New Approach to the Economic Analysis of Nonstationary Time Series and the Business Cycle*  
**Publication:** Econometrica, 57(2), 357–384.

**Core concept / belief:** Markets are not generated by one permanent distribution. An unobserved finite-state Markov process can switch between regimes, and the current state must be inferred probabilistically.

**Theoretical basis:** The Hamilton filter recursively calculates filtered state probabilities, while the transition matrix provides multi-step persistence and transition probabilities.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Preserve the existing higher-standard regime as the common Field 3/Field 10 regime identity.
- Publish selected-state probability, second-state probability, posterior margin, entropy, self-transition probability, and 1H/3H/6H/24H transition risk.
- Use regime persistence as evidence for a whole-day bias, not as a standalone entry signal.

**Benefit:** Reduces unstable direction changes and makes the whole-day bias depend on a probabilistic regime rather than one indicator threshold.

---

## Paper 2 — Jushan Bai and Pierre Perron (1998)

**Exact title:** *Estimating and Testing Linear Models with Multiple Structural Changes*  
**Publication:** Econometrica, 66(1), 47–78.

**Core concept / belief:** A long historical window is useful only when the generating relationship has not structurally changed.

**Theoretical basis:** Multiple unknown breakpoints can be estimated globally and tested statistically under serial dependence.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Detect breaks in trend slope, volatility, spread behavior, volume behavior, session coefficients, and news-response coefficients.
- Add `Last Structural Break`, `Break Strength`, `Post-Break H1 Count`, and `Post-Break Validation Permission`.
- If a severe break has fewer than the configured minimum post-break observations, downgrade entry permission and do not treat older observations as equal evidence.

**Benefit:** Prevents the last 600 candles from producing false confidence when a large recent market change makes older candles less relevant.

---

## Paper 3 — Ryan Prescott Adams and David J. C. MacKay (2007)

**Exact title:** *Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection*  
**Publication:** arXiv:0710.3742.

**Core concept / belief:** The system should maintain a posterior distribution over the time since the most recent change, instead of using one fixed regime-age number.

**Theoretical basis:** Bayesian message passing recursively estimates the posterior run-length distribution and current changepoint probability.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Calculate changepoint probability from completed H1 observations only.
- Add `BOCPD Change Probability`, `Modal Run Length`, `Run-Length Uncertainty`, and `Regime Invalidating Evidence`.
- A high changepoint probability may downgrade ALLOW to CAUTION/BLOCK, but it must not rewrite the locked morning bias intraday.

**Benefit:** Protects the user when the morning regime begins to fail later in the day.

## Paper 4 — Torben G. Andersen and Tim Bollerslev (1997)

**Exact title:** *Intraday Periodicity and Volatility Persistence in Financial Markets*  
**Publication:** Journal of Empirical Finance, 4(2–3), 115–158.

**Core concept / belief:** Intraday volatility has strong periodic structure. Comparing London volatility directly with a quiet rollover hour without seasonal adjustment is misleading.

**Theoretical basis:** Intraday periodic components can be separated from persistent volatility dynamics so that session comparisons are made on a normalized basis.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Estimate hour-of-week volatility seasonality from completed historical observations.
- Normalize current volatility, spread, tick volume, and expected movement by session baseline.
- Add `Session-Normalized Volatility`, `Session Volatility Percentile`, and `Abnormal Activity Score`.

**Benefit:** Makes the eight-session ranking fair and prevents active sessions from being penalized simply because their normal volatility is higher.

## Paper 5 — Michel M. Dacorogna, Ulrich A. Müller, Robert J. Nagler, Richard B. Olsen, and Olivier V. Pictet (1993)

**Exact title:** *A Geographical Model for the Daily and Weekly Seasonal Volatility in the Foreign Exchange Market*  
**Publication:** Journal of International Money and Finance, 12(4), 413–438.

**Core concept / belief:** FX volatility is generated by changing and overlapping participation from Asia, Europe, and America.

**Theoretical basis:** Geographical market components explain recurring daily and weekly FX volatility patterns and their overlaps.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Build eight dynamic session windows using market-local time zones and daylight-saving rules, then convert them to the shared broker time.
- Estimate each symbol’s historical behavior in each session and overlap.
- Add session compatibility, overlap advantage, and best-session rankings.

**Benefit:** Produces a genuine FX-specific session table instead of assigning one generic session label to every symbol.

## Paper 6 — Glenn W. Brier (1950)

**Exact title:** *Verification of Forecasts Expressed in Terms of Probability*  
**Publication:** Monthly Weather Review, 78(1), 1–3.

**Core concept / belief:** A probability is useful only when forecasts declared near a probability occur at approximately that frequency.

**Theoretical basis:** The Brier score is a proper scoring rule for probabilistic forecasts.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Store immutable 1H/3H/6H directional and event-impact probabilities before outcomes are known.
- Settle forecasts without overwriting the original prediction.
- Add `Brier Score`, `Reliability Error`, `Resolution`, `Calibration Sample`, and `Calibration Status`.

**Benefit:** Stops confidence, reliability, and probability from being treated as interchangeable values.

## Paper 7 — Alexandru Niculescu-Mizil and Rich Caruana (2005)

**Exact title:** *Predicting Good Probabilities with Supervised Learning*  
**Publication:** Proceedings of the 22nd International Conference on Machine Learning.

**Core concept / belief:** Good rank discrimination does not guarantee good probability estimates; post-model probability calibration is often necessary.

**Theoretical basis:** Platt scaling and isotonic regression can correct systematic probability distortion, subject to out-of-sample calibration data.

**Field 10 implementation:**

- Select calibration method only through walk-forward validation.
- Add `Raw Probability`, `Calibrated Probability`, `Calibration Method`, and `Expected Calibration Error`.

**Benefit:** Makes “70%” mean something empirically testable rather than merely a normalized score.

## Paper 8 — Yaniv Romano, Evan Patterson, and Emmanuel J. Candès (2019)

**Exact title:** *Conformalized Quantile Regression*  
**Publication:** Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 32; arXiv:1905.03222.

**Core concept / belief:** Prediction intervals should widen in difficult, volatile situations and narrow in stable situations while maintaining empirical coverage.

**Theoretical basis:** Quantile regression combined with conformal calibration provides finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Calculate lower/median/upper return estimates for each relevant horizon and session.
- Calibrate on a purged rolling calibration window.
- Add `Conformal Lower`, `Conformal Upper`, `Interval Width`, `Coverage`, and `Coverage Error`.

**Benefit:** Improves uncertainty, expected-return ranges, and risk-aware session decisions.

## Paper 9 — Isaac Gibbs and Emmanuel Candès (2021)

**Exact title:** *Adaptive Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift*  
**Publication:** Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 34; arXiv:2106.00170.

**Core concept / belief:** Fixed calibration can fail when market conditions shift; the interval calibration level must adapt online.

**Theoretical basis:** Adaptive conformal inference updates a calibration parameter over time to maintain long-run target coverage under distribution shift.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Maintain adaptive coverage states by symbol, horizon, and optionally session.
- Widen uncertainty or downgrade permission when recent miscoverage rises.
- Add `Adaptive Coverage State`, `Recent Miscoverage`, and `Distribution-Shift Warning`.

**Benefit:** Prevents a historically calibrated model from appearing safe after a rapid volatility or regime shift.

## Paper 10 — Peter Reinhard Hansen (2005)

**Exact title:** *A Test for Superior Predictive Ability*  
**Publication:** Journal of Business & Economic Statistics, 23(4), 365–380.

**Core concept / belief:** Trying many ranking formulas makes a lucky winner likely; a candidate must demonstrate genuine superiority over a benchmark.

**Theoretical basis:** The SPA test uses studentized loss differentials and bootstrap inference while reducing data-snooping distortion.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Register the current production score as the benchmark and the expanded 20-paper score as a shadow candidate.
- Compare settled loss after spread and risk penalties.
- Store candidate count, bootstrap draws, block length, p-value, and experiment hash.

**Benefit:** Prevents the new score from replacing the current ranking merely because it looks better on the same data used to design it.

## Paper 11 — David H. Bailey, Jonathan M. Borwein, Marcos López de Prado, and Qiji Jim Zhu (2017)

**Exact title:** *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting*  
**Publication:** Journal of Computational Finance, 20(4), 39–69; earlier working-paper version SSRN 2326253.

**Core concept / belief:** The best in-sample configuration may be the configuration most fitted to noise.

**Theoretical basis:** Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation estimates the probability that an in-sample winner underperforms out of sample.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Keep the full candidate-performance matrix, not only the winning configuration.
- Calculate PBO for score weights, thresholds, session definitions, and event half-life choices.
- Block promotion when PBO is above the configured governance threshold.

**Benefit:** Reduces false improvements caused by repeatedly tuning the 600-candle model.

## Paper 12 — David H. Bailey and Marcos López de Prado (2014)

**Exact title:** *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality*  
**Publication:** Journal of Portfolio Management, 40(5), 94–107.

**Core concept / belief:** A high observed performance statistic is not impressive when many alternatives were tested or returns are non-normal.

**Theoretical basis:** The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts performance significance for selection bias, multiple trials, skewness, and kurtosis.

**Field 10 implementation:**
- Calculate DSR for the new whole-day candidate and session candidate using settled spread-adjusted returns.
- Store number of trials, skewness, kurtosis, observed Sharpe, deflated probability, and minimum track record length.
- Do not promote until the candidate has a sufficient history and acceptable DSR.

**Benefit:** Adds a stricter promotion gate beyond ordinary win rate and average return.

**Publication:** The Journal of Risk, 2(3), 21–41.

**Core concept / belief:** “Less risky” should measure the average severity of the worst losses, not only volatility or win probability.

**Theoretical basis:** CVaR can be represented and optimized through a convex auxiliary objective over loss scenarios.

- Use CVaR to downgrade permission and position size, never to force direction.

**Benefit:** Prevents high-win-rate symbols with severe occasional losses from ranking as the safest choices.

# 3. Recommended Field 10 architecture

## 3.1 Non-negotiable daily behavior

- Use exactly the last **600 completed H1 candles** for the current morning whole-day state calculation.
- Never use the forming candle.
- Use the shared broker candle identity and shared broker time provider.
- Publish one immutable `daily_snapshot_id` and one ordered symbol universe.
- Keep rank, whole-day bias, score, and top-four order unchanged until the broker-day review.
- A live safety overlay may only change `Live Safety Status`, `Entry Permission`, and `Safety Reason` from ALLOW to CAUTION/BLOCK. It must never rerank or reverse the locked whole-day bias.
- At the end-of-day review, settle outcomes, preserve the original forecast, and prepare the next-day candidate.

### Important validation distinction

The current state may be calculated from the latest 600 completed H1 candles, but validation and promotion should use the longest clean archive available—preferably thousands of completed H1 candles. Twenty research layers cannot be responsibly tuned and validated on only 600 observations.

## 3.3 Shadow candidate whole-day score

Do not immediately replace the current production score. Register this as `field10_whole_day_candidate_v1` and validate it.

### Eligibility gates

A symbol is not eligible for a normal rank when any hard gate fails:

1. Fewer than 600 valid completed H1 candles.
2. Candle identity mismatch or stale parent/child generation.
3. Severe structural break with insufficient post-break observations.
4. Critical data-quality failure.
5. Spread or execution hard block.
6. Safety veto.
7. Calibration/validation evidence explicitly failed.

Missing optional evidence must not become zero. Reweight only across available noncritical components and display an evidence-completeness penalty.

### Initial shadow score registry

Use these only as initial research weights, not as proven production weights:

```
WholeDayScore =
    0.18 * RegimePersistence
  + 0.14 * CalibratedBiasProbability
  + 0.13 * SessionRobustness
  + 0.13 * RiskAdjustedEV
  + 0.10 * TailSafety
  + 0.09 * ExecutionQuality
  + 0.08 * NewsAbsorptionSafety
  + 0.08 * UncertaintySafety
  + 0.07 * UniqueExposureScore
  - TransitionPenalty
  - ConflictPenalty
  - ChangepointPenalty
  - ResidualNewsShockPenalty
```

All inputs are normalized to 0–100 with a versioned formula registry. Store every component and penalty separately. Never hide a penalty inside the final score.

### Whole-day bias decision

- Determine direction from the shared Field 3 higher-standard bias plus probabilistic regime and calibrated directional evidence.
- Require cross-source agreement for `LOCKED_BUY` or `LOCKED_SELL`.
- Use `NEUTRAL/WAIT` when the directional posterior margin, calibrated probability, or cross-session agreement is insufficient.
- A whole-day bias is a directional preference, not permission to enter during every session.

## Impact and absorption method

1. Define event time `t0` from the real source timestamp.
2. Match historical events using event type, affected currency, surprise bucket, session, volatility regime, and structural-break segment.
3. Estimate the historical event response path using only completed observations that were fully in the past at the current cutoff.
4. Calculate current abnormal return and cumulative abnormal return relative to a session/regime baseline.
5. Estimate event intensity with a Hawkes model when samples are sufficient; otherwise use a versioned exponential-decay fallback.
6. Calculate:

```
TimeDecayRemaining = exp(-ln(2) * EventAge / EstimatedHalfLife)
```

7. Combine time decay with historical response completion, current abnormal return, abnormal volatility, volume confirmation, and flow-proxy confirmation.
8. Keep `Impact Remaining` and `Absorption Percentage` bounded between 0 and 100.

### Absorption labels

- `UNABSORBED`: absorption below 35%
- `PARTIALLY_ABSORBED`: 35% to below 70%
- `MOSTLY_ABSORBED`: 70% to below 90%
- `ABSORBED`: at least 90%, residual intensity low, and no new related event cluster
- `UNAVAILABLE`: insufficient or untrusted timestamp/event evidence

These thresholds must be stored in a registry and validated, not buried in UI code.

# 11. Final warning about interpretation

A stable whole-day bias does not mean entering at any hour is safe. It means the directional preference is locked for the day. The eight-session table and live safety overlay determine whether the current session is suitable for entry. When evidence is weak, conflicted, affected by an unabsorbed high-impact event, or structurally unstable, the correct institutional output is `WAIT`, `CAUTION`, or `BLOCK`—not a fabricated BUY or SELL.
